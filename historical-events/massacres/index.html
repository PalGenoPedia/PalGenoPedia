  1. 1948-04-09Deir YassinWar Crime (1907 Hague)Massacre · ≈107–250 Deaths · ≈710 DisplacedThe Deir Yassin massacre occurred during intense fighting around Jerusalem in early April 1948 (Operation Nachshon context). The village had previously concluded a local non-aggression understanding with nearby Jewish communities; nonetheless, Irgun and Lehi launched a multi-axis assault at dawn. Haganah/Palmach elements later provided support.
  2. 1948-05-22 – 1948-05-23TanturaWar Crime (1907 Hague)Massacre · ≈200-250 Deaths · ≈1,500 DisplacedOn May 22-23, 1948, the Alexandroni Brigade of the Haganah (which became the IDF) attacked the Palestinian coastal village of Tantura. The village had a population of approximately 1,500 and was known for its fishing industry. Despite attempts at surrender and raising white flags, Israeli forces conducted house-to-house searches, separated men from women and children, and executed male villagers.
  3. 1948-07-09 – 1948-07-13Lydda and RamleWar CrimeMassacre, Forced Expulsion, Death March · ≈400–1,000 Deaths · ≈50,000–70,000 DisplacedOn July 9–13, 1948, during Operation Dani under the command of Yigal Allon (with Yitzhak Rabin as deputy), Israeli forces captured Lydda and Ramle. In Lydda, city-wide shootings killed hundreds of civilians; the Dahmash Mosque massacre alone claimed 70–250+ lives (estimates vary). Moshe Dayan's 89th Battalion participated in ground operations.
  4. 1953-10-14QibyaWar CrimeMassacre · 69 Deaths · Temporary evacuation during attack DisplacedThe Qibya massacre occurred during heightened tensions along the Jordan-Israel armistice line. Following the October 12, 1953 killing of an Israeli woman and her two children in Yehud, Israel launched 'Operation Shoshana'—a large-scale reprisal attack on the Jordanian village of Qibya.
  5. 1956-11-03Khan Yunis MassacreWar CrimeMassacre · 275-525 Deaths · Temporary displacement during operations DisplacedIn October-November 1956, Israel launched Operation Kadesh in coordination with France and the UK's invasion of Egypt during the Suez Crisis. Israeli forces rapidly occupied the Gaza Strip and Sinai Peninsula. During the occupation of Gaza, IDF conducted systematic 'screening' operations ostensibly to identify Egyptian soldiers and Palestinian fighters (fedayeen) who had infiltrated Israel.
  6. 1967-06-05Six-Day War / Al-NaksaWar CrimesMass Displacement · 10,000-15,000 combatants total (all sides); civilian deaths not systematically recorded Deaths · 300,000-400,000 Palestinians DisplacedThe Six-Day War erupted on June 5, 1967 when Israel launched preemptive strikes on Egyptian airfields, followed by ground operations. Within six days, Israel occupied the Egyptian-controlled Gaza Strip and Sinai Peninsula, Jordanian-controlled West Bank and East Jerusalem, and Syrian Golan Heights. The rapid Israeli victory fundamentally transformed the Israeli-Palestinian conflict by bringing all of historic Palestine under Israeli control.
  7. 1982-09-16Sabra and ShatilaWar CrimeMassacre · 800-3,500 Deaths · Survivors fled camps; infrastructure destroyed DisplacedThe Sabra and Shatila massacre occurred during Israel's 1982 invasion of Lebanon ('Operation Peace for Galilee'). After the assassination of Lebanese President-elect Bachir Gemayel on September 14, 1982, Israeli Defense Minister Ariel Sharon ordered IDF forces to occupy West Beirut, ostensibly to maintain order. On September 16, Sharon authorized Lebanese Phalangist militias to enter the Sabra and Shatila Palestinian refugee camps, claiming they would 'mop up' remaining PLO fighters—though PLO forces had evacuated weeks earlier under international agreement.
  8. 1987-12-08First IntifadaMass Casualties / UprisingMass Casualties / Uprising · 1,087-1,284 Palestinians; 160-200 Israelis Deaths · Thousands displaced through home demolitions DisplacedOn December 8, 1987, an Israeli army truck collided with civilian vehicles at the Erez checkpoint in Gaza, killing four Palestinian workers from Jabalia refugee camp. Palestinians believed it was deliberate revenge for the stabbing death of an Israeli in Gaza two days earlier. The following day, December 9, massive protests erupted in Jabalia. When 17-year-old Hatem al-Sisi was shot dead by Israeli soldiers, the uprising exploded across Gaza, East Jerusalem, and the West Bank within days. What began as spontaneous protests transformed into organized, sustained civil resistance coordinated by grassroots committees and later by the United National Leadership of the Uprising (UNLU).
  9. 1994-02-25Cave of the Patriarchs MassacreWar CrimeMassacre / Mass Shooting · 50-60 Palestinians total (29 in mosque shooting + 25+ killed by Israeli forces during protests) Deaths · Mass exodus from Hebron Old City due to closures and restrictions DisplacedBaruch Goldstein was born Benjamin Carl Goldstein in Brooklyn, New York in 1956 to an Orthodox Jewish family. In the 1970s, he became a charter member of the Jewish Defense League (JDL), a militant organization founded by Rabbi Meir Kahane. After graduating from Yeshiva University's Albert Einstein College of Medicine, Goldstein emigrated to Israel in 1983. He served as a physician in the Israel Defense Forces and later settled in Kiryat Arba, an extremist settlement adjacent to Hebron. Goldstein was an active member of Kach, Kahane's ultranationalist party, and was third on its list for the 1984 Knesset elections. Israeli press reported that Goldstein refused to treat Arabs, even Arab soldiers serving in the IDF, believing it violated Jewish religious law to treat non-Jews. He compared Israel's democracy to Nazism and wore a yellow star marked 'JUDE' in protest. In the weeks before the massacre, Goldstein made ominous statements and was involved in violent incidents at the mosque, including pouring acid on carpets. Palestinian authorities wrote to Prime Minister Rabin warning about Goldstein's dangerous behavior, but no action was taken.
  10. 2000-09-28Second IntifadaWar CrimesArmed Uprising / Mass Casualties · 3,200-4,800 Palestinians; 1,000-1,100 Israelis Deaths · Thousands displaced through home demolitions and military operations DisplacedThe Second Intifada's immediate trigger was Ariel Sharon's September 28, 2000 visit to the Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif, accompanied by 1,000 Israeli police. Palestinians viewed this as deliberate provocation at one of Islam's holiest sites by a figure they associated with past massacres (Sabra and Shatila 1982, Qibya 1953). Protests erupted immediately. The next day, September 29 (a Friday), after prayers at Al-Aqsa Mosque, Israeli police fired on Palestinian demonstrators, killing 7 and wounding over 300. By October 1, violence had spread throughout the occupied territories and into Israel. Thirteen Palestinian citizens of Israel were shot dead by Israeli police during protests inside Israel proper. Within two weeks, 100 Palestinians and 10 Israelis had been killed. The uprising's deeper causes lay in the failure of the Oslo peace process: seven years after Oslo, Palestinians remained under occupation, settlements had doubled, movement restrictions had increased, and statehood seemed further away than ever.
  11. 2002-04-03Battle of JeninWar Crimes (HRW prima facie)Military Operation / Urban Warfare · 52 Palestinians (22 civilians, 27 combatants, 3 unclear); 23 Israeli soldiers Deaths · 4,000 Palestinians rendered homeless DisplacedJenin refugee camp was established in 1953 to house Palestinians displaced during the 1948 Nakba, primarily from Haifa and surrounding villages. By 2002, approximately 14,000 refugees lived in the densely packed camp covering just one square kilometer. Under the Oslo Accords, Jenin came under full Palestinian Authority control in 1995 (Area A). However, the camp became a stronghold for Palestinian militant groups during the Second Intifada. According to both Israeli and Palestinian sources, an estimated 80-100 armed fighters from Hamas, Islamic Jihad, Al-Aqsa Martyrs' Brigades, and Tanzim operated from the camp. Israel claimed 28 suicide attacks between October 2000 and April 2002 were planned and launched from Jenin. After the devastating Passover massacre at Park Hotel in Netanya killed 30 Israeli civilians on March 27, 2002, the Israeli government launched Operation Defensive Shield—the largest military operation in the West Bank since 1967. Jenin refugee camp was identified as a primary target.
  12. 2008-12-27Operation Cast LeadWar CrimesMilitary Operation · 1,383-1,419 Palestinians; 13 Israelis (10 soldiers, 3 civilians) Deaths · 20,000+ Palestinians made homeless DisplacedOperation Cast Lead began at 11:30 AM on December 27, 2008, when Israeli F-16 fighter jets and Apache helicopters simultaneously struck over 100 pre-planned targets across Gaza within 220 seconds. The opening attacks hit police stations—including during a graduation ceremony for new officers, killing 99 police personnel—government buildings, and Hamas infrastructure. By the end of the first day, at least 230 Palestinians were killed.
  13. 2012-11-14Operation Pillar of DefenseWar CrimesMilitary Operation, Airstrikes, Civilian Casualties · 167 Palestinian, 6 Israeli Deaths · Not documented; blockade reinforced post-operation DisplacedOn November 14, 2012, at approximately 15:45, the Israeli Air Force assassinated Ahmed al-Jabari, head of Hamas's military wing, marking the start of Operation Pillar of Defense. Over eight days, the IDF struck approximately 1,500 targets across Gaza—including underground rocket launchers, arms-smuggling tunnels, weapons storage facilities, and residences of Hamas officials. Unlike Operation Cast Lead (2008-09), no ground incursion occurred.
  14. 2014-07-07Operation Protective EdgeWar CrimesMilitary Operation · 2,251 Palestinians (1,462 civilians, 551 children, 299 women); 73 Israelis (67 soldiers, 6 civilians) Deaths · 500,000 Palestinians (1/3 of Gaza's population); 100,000 remained displaced one year later DisplacedOperation Protective Edge began on July 7, 2014, ostensibly in response to escalating rocket fire from Gaza, but in a context of heightened tensions following the abduction and murder of three Israeli teenagers in the West Bank in June. Israel launched massive airstrikes across Gaza, conducting over 6,000 air raids in 51 days. The operation had three phases: an initial week of intensive aerial bombardment, a ground invasion beginning July 17 aimed at destroying cross-border tunnels, and a final phase from August 5 characterized by alternating ceasefires and continued strikes. The stated objectives were to stop rocket attacks and destroy Hamas's military infrastructure and tunnel network.
  15. 2018-03-30Great March of ReturnMass Killings / Protest SuppressionMass Killings / Protest Suppression · 223 Palestinians (189 during 2018; 34 during 2019); Zero Israelis killed Deaths · No displacement but severe long-term disabilities created DisplacedThe Great March of Return began on March 30, 2018—Palestinian Land Day and the 42nd anniversary of mass protests against Israeli land confiscation. Organized by independent activists, the protests were conceived as peaceful demonstrations to draw international attention to Gaza's humanitarian crisis, the blockade, and the right of return for Palestinian refugees. Five designated protest sites were established along Gaza's eastern border, several hundred meters from the separation fence. The protests were intended to last from Land Day (March 30) through Nakba Day (May 15), but continued for 21 months until Hamas announced their end on December 27, 2019.
  16. 2021-05-10Operation Guardian of the WallsMilitary OperationMilitary Operation · 260 Palestinians (129 civilians, 66 children, 40 women); 13 Israelis (12 civilians, 1 soldier) Deaths · Tens of thousands temporarily displaced in Gaza DisplacedThe escalation that became Operation Guardian of the Walls was rooted in events in Jerusalem. During Ramadan 2021, Israeli police erected barriers preventing Palestinians from gathering at Damascus Gate—a traditional gathering place—and violently suppressed protests. Simultaneously, Israeli courts moved forward with eviction proceedings against Palestinian families in the Sheikh Jarrah neighborhood of East Jerusalem to make way for Jewish settlers. On May 7, hundreds of Palestinians were injured when Israeli police stormed Al-Aqsa Mosque compound during prayers, firing rubber bullets, stun grenades, and tear gas inside the mosque itself. International condemnation was swift, but on May 10—Lailat al-Qadr, the holiest night of Ramadan—Israeli police again raided Al-Aqsa, injuring over 300 Palestinians. Hamas issued an ultimatum demanding Israeli forces withdraw from Al-Aqsa and Sheikh Jarrah by 6 PM. When Israel refused, Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad fired rockets toward Jerusalem at 6:05 PM, marking the beginning of the conflict.
  17. 2022-01-01West Bank Raids 2022–2023Unlawful killingsMilitary Raids / Systematic Killings · 500+ Palestinians (2022-2023 combined) including 78+ children; 19 Israelis killed by Palestinians (early 2022) Deaths · Multiple communities displaced by settler violence; 500 families displaced in Jenin July 2023 DisplacedThe escalation began in spring 2022 following a series of attacks by Palestinians inside Israel that killed 19 Israelis between March 22 and May 7. Israel responded by launching 'Operation Break the Wave'—near-nightly military raids into West Bank cities and refugee camps, particularly Jenin and Nablus. These cities had become centers of renewed armed Palestinian resistance with the emergence of groups like the Jenin Brigades (founded September 2021), the Lions' Den in Nablus, and the Balata Brigades. The Israeli operations involved massive deployments of troops, bulldozers destroying infrastructure and roads, use of live ammunition in densely populated areas, and increasingly, aerial bombardment including drone strikes. By the end of 2022, 220 Palestinians had been killed including 48 children—the highest annual toll since the Second Intifada ended in 2005.
